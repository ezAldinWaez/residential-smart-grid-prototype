Conclusions and Suggestions
===========================
Conclusions
-----------
The Residential Smart Grid Prototype serves as both a practical solution to a real problem and a research tool that collects data and visualizes it to facilitate research and development of its algorithms. Using standard libraries like pvlib and pvwatts and international data sources like NSRDB, and implementing smart algorithms that are based on statistics, RSGP has demonstrated great results and showed potential for real world application to fairly distribute the generated solar power without wasting resources and efficiently manage the shared battery to meet each house's demand.

Suggestions
-----------
- **Machine Learning Integration**: Machine learning algorithms could greatly enhance power management when implemented for solar power generation prediction, demand forecasting, and virtual battery allocation optimizations.
- **Enhanced Visualization Capabilities**: Research productivity could improve through more sophisticated visualization tools, including real-time performance dashboards.
- **Commercial Building Integration**: RSGP could see broader application if extended to include commercial and industrial loads.
- **Economic Modeling and Market Simulation**: The current implementation of demand response incentive could imrpove through adding economic models for electricity pricing and energy trading.

These suggestions are based on what we currently see RSGP to be lacking. However, employing RSGP in the real world could provide insights into the human response to it, and thus provide feedback on the weak points of RSGP and allow for a clearer view on how to further develop the project. As such, the current priority of the project should be real-world prototyping, after which the aforementioned suggestions should be re-evaluated and possibly integrated.
